Abstract

Simulation is an indispensable tool for evaluating robot policies in a safe and scalable manner. Current simulation benchmarks run robot policies synchronously, pausing the simulation to let the policy “think” for as long as it needs before executing actions. However, real world physics does not wait for policies. Synchronous benchmarks introduce an artificial sim-to-real gap by providing an unfair advantage to heavyweight policies with large backbones or multi-pass inference that may be performant, but are too slow in the real world.

In this work, we show how any synchronous robotic simulation benchmark can be made asynchronous by running the simulator and policy in parallel threads. The simulator is run in realtime and communicates with the policy via inter-process queues for observations and actions. Importantly, the rate at which the simulator runs is decoupled from the rate at which the policy consumes observations and produces actions.

We instantiate our approach in robosuite, which is a building block for multiple robotic simulation benchmarks, thereby automatically making these benchmarks asynchronous. We show that performance in asynchronous simulation of various benchmark robotics tasks is significantly more correlated with real-world performance than synchronous simulation, providing a simple and effective way to reduce the sim-to-real gap of existing simulation benchmarks.
